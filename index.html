What a Certificate of Analysis (COA) Tells You About a Supplement

A Certificate of Analysis — COA — is a lab report for one specific batch of a product. It lists what was tested, how it was tested, and whether the results met the specification. For buyers, it is the closest thing to seeing inside the bottle before you buy it.

A credible COA names the batch or lot number, the test date, and the laboratory. Independent third-party labs matter more than in-house testing, because the lab has no stake in a pass. Look for identity testing (is the ingredient really what the label says?), potency results with actual measured numbers rather than a bare "pass", and contaminant screens for heavy metals and microbes.

Three checks separate a real COA from window dressing. First, the batch number must match the bottle you are buying — a 2023 COA says nothing about the current lot. Second, the report should show measured values, not just pass/fail. Third, the lab should be accredited; ISO 17025 is the standard to look for.

Watch for red flags: undated COAs, COAs covering a single ingredient of a multi-ingredient formula, and brands that advertise "third-party tested" but will not show the document. A COA proves what is in the bottle, not that the product works — honest brands say so plainly.
